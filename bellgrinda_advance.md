# BellGrinda Advanced Progression Framework

**Version:** 0.1  
**Training Level:** Advanced  
**Structure Type:** Adaptive Framework  
**Default Block Length:** 6–9 weeks  
**Phases:** 3  
**Phase Duration:** Dynamic, generally 2–3 weeks each  
**Training Days:** Monday–Friday  
**Session Duration:** Approximately 40 minutes  
**Primary Equipment:** 12 kg, 16 kg, 20 kg kettlebells where available  
**Secondary Equipment:** Bodyweight, skipping rope, mat, chair/bench  
**Programming Model:** Data-driven and exercise-specific

---

# 1. Advanced Level Purpose

Advanced marks the point where BellGrinda no longer relies primarily on prewritten training templates.

The purpose of Advanced is to convert accumulated historical training data into individualized training blocks.

The system should use:

- Novice history.
- Advanced Novice history.
- Intermediate history.
- Performance tests.
- Biweekly assessments.
- Exercise-specific load progression.
- Push-up progression.
- RPE trends.
- Training consistency.
- Pain/discomfort history.
- Football workload.
- Jogging workload.
- Readiness trends.
- Available kettlebells.
- Personal Records.
- Reinforcement-block history.

Intermediate explicitly prepares BellGrinda for this transition by collecting these data categories.

Advanced should therefore answer:

**What does this user now actually need?**

rather than:

**What normally comes after Intermediate?**

---

# 2. Advanced Philosophy

Advanced progression shall prioritize:

1. Individual weak-point development.
2. Exercise-specific strength.
3. Athletic power.
4. Conditioning quality.
5. Work capacity.
6. Load progression.
7. Recovery management.
8. Movement quality.
9. Training density.
10. Sustainable long-term performance.

The goal is not simply:

- more reps;
- more rounds;
- heavier weight;
- less rest.

Advanced should use the smallest effective change required to continue progress.

---

# 3. Advanced Entry Analysis

Before creating the first Advanced block, BellGrinda shall perform an **Advanced Entry Review**.

The review shall analyze the user's previous training history.

Required categories:

- Consistency.
- Strength.
- Push performance.
- Pull performance.
- Lower-body performance.
- Hinge performance.
- Conditioning.
- Unilateral control.
- Complex performance.
- Recovery.
- Pain/discomfort.
- Football workload.
- Jogging workload.
- Equipment availability.

BellGrinda shall identify:

- strongest areas;
- weakest areas;
- stalled areas;
- recently progressing areas;
- high-fatigue areas;
- movements requiring caution.

---

# 4. Advanced Training Profile

BellGrinda shall generate an Advanced Training Profile.

Example:

**Strength:** Strong  
**Conditioning:** Competent  
**Push Strength:** Developing  
**Pull Strength:** Excellent  
**Lower Body:** Strong  
**Power:** Competent  
**Recovery:** Developing  
**Consistency:** Excellent  
**Swing Status:** Flagged  
**Football Load:** High

This profile becomes the basis for programming.

---

# 5. Priority Selection

Each Advanced block shall have:

## One Primary Priority

Examples:

- Strength.
- Conditioning.
- Upper-body pushing.
- Lower-body strength.
- Power.
- Work capacity.
- Complex performance.
- Recovery quality.

## One Secondary Priority

Examples:

- Push-up development.
- Unilateral strength.
- Conditioning.
- Load progression.
- Movement technique.

## Maintenance Areas

Areas already performing well should receive enough work to maintain capacity without consuming unnecessary recovery.

---

# 6. Example Priority Model

Example user profile:

Strong:

- Goblet squat.
- Row.
- Conditioning.

Developing:

- Overhead press.
- Push-up strength.

Recurring issue:

- Swing discomfort.

BellGrinda may create:

**Primary Priority:** Pressing strength  
**Secondary Priority:** Chest development  
**Maintenance:** Lower-body strength and conditioning  
**Restricted:** Ballistic hinge work

The resulting program should look materially different from that of another Advanced user with poor conditioning but strong pressing.

---

# 7. Adaptive Block Duration

Advanced does not require one fixed level duration.

Default block length:

**6–9 weeks**

A block may consist of:

## Phase 1 — Build

Approximately 2–3 weeks.

## Phase 2 — Intensify

Approximately 2–3 weeks.

## Phase 3 — Consolidate/Test

Approximately 2–3 weeks.

The exact duration may depend on:

- performance trend;
- consistency;
- fatigue;
- football competition schedule;
- equipment changes;
- reinforcement needs.

Training Level remains Advanced throughout repeated blocks.

---

# 8. Advanced Phase 1 — Build

The Build Phase establishes the priority-specific workload.

Focus:

- establish new working loads;
- establish exercise variations;
- moderate RPE;
- maintain strong movement quality;
- establish baseline density.

Target RPE:

**6–7**

The Build Phase should not begin with maximal progression.

---

# 9. Advanced Phase 2 — Intensify

Focus:

- increased load;
- increased complexity;
- increased density;
- stronger unilateral emphasis;
- more difficult push-up variations;
- stronger complex integration.

Target RPE:

**7–8**

BellGrinda should allow occasional difficult sessions without normalizing constant maximal effort.

---

# 10. Advanced Phase 3 — Consolidate

Focus:

- stabilize gains;
- verify progression;
- reduce unnecessary variation;
- prepare for assessment;
- identify the next adaptive priority.

Target RPE:

**6–8**

The final week may reduce fatigue slightly before testing.

---

# 11. Weekly Structure

Advanced shall retain the familiar Monday–Friday framework but allow the focus of each day to change according to the current priority.

Default architecture:

## Monday

Primary strength emphasis.

## Tuesday

Power / conditioning.

## Wednesday

Upper-body / chest / secondary priority.

## Thursday

Complex / work-capacity day.

## Friday

Football-aware adaptive session.

The structure remains recognizable while internal prescriptions become more individualized.

---

# 12. Dynamic Monday

Monday shall focus on the current primary strength requirement.

Possible movement categories:

- Heavy goblet squat.
- Front-rack squat.
- Split squat.
- Heavy row.
- Press.
- Floor press.
- Deadlift.
- Loaded carry/march.

BellGrinda shall select five stations based on:

- current priority;
- equipment;
- exercise status;
- previous performance.

Example strength-priority session:

1. 20 kg goblet squat — 8–10.
2. 20 kg row — 8 per arm.
3. 16 kg overhead press — 5 per arm.
4. 16 kg reverse lunge — 8 per leg.
5. 20 kg suitcase march — 30 steps per side.

Complete:

**5 rounds**

where appropriate.

---

# 13. Dynamic Tuesday

Tuesday shall emphasize:

- power;
- athletic conditioning;
- ballistic skills;
- lower-body athleticism.

Possible exercises:

- Swing.
- Clean.
- Snatch.
- Clean to front squat.
- Front-rack lunge.
- Skipping.
- Explosive push-up variation.

Ballistic movements shall only appear if technique and pain history permit.

---

# 14. Dynamic Wednesday

Wednesday remains the primary chest and upper-body development day.

Possible exercises:

- Overhead press.
- Floor press.
- Row.
- Decline push-up.
- Tempo push-up.
- Pause push-up.
- Advanced push-up variation.
- Core anti-rotation/stability work.

BellGrinda shall maintain balance between:

- chest development;
- pushing strength;
- shoulder recovery;
- total weekly pressing volume.

---

# 15. Dynamic Thursday

Thursday remains the primary complex / work-capacity day.

Possible structures:

- Clean → press → squat.
- Clean → reverse lunge → press.
- Swing → clean → squat.
- Snatch → reverse lunge where technically appropriate.

Advanced complexes may involve:

- multiple movements;
- mixed unilateral work;
- heavier kettlebells;
- lower repetitions;
- tighter transitions.

Complexes should remain technically controlled.

---

# 16. Dynamic Friday

Friday remains governed by football status.

Possible modes:

- Normal.
- Football scheduled.
- Competition.
- Post-match recovery.
- High-fatigue week.

BellGrinda shall continue treating football as meaningful athletic workload rather than an unrelated activity.

---

# 17. Exercise Selection Engine

Advanced exercise selection shall use tags.

Each exercise may be tagged by:

- movement pattern;
- training effect;
- difficulty;
- load suitability;
- unilateral/bilateral status;
- conditioning demand;
- technical demand;
- pain restrictions;
- equipment requirement.

Example tags:

**Goblet Squat**

- squat;
- strength;
- lower body;
- bilateral;
- low technical demand;
- 12/16/20 kg suitable.

BellGrinda can then construct sessions according to need rather than hard-coded exercise order.

---

# 18. Movement-Balance Rules

BellGrinda should generally maintain weekly exposure to:

- Squat.
- Hinge.
- Push.
- Pull.
- Unilateral lower-body.
- Core/stability.
- Conditioning.

The exact number of exposures may vary.

A priority block may temporarily emphasize one category but should not completely neglect major movement patterns without reason.

---

# 19. Advanced Load Management

BellGrinda shall support different kettlebell weights simultaneously.

Example:

Deadlift:
**20 kg**

Goblet squat:
**20 kg**

Row:
**20 kg**

Clean:
**16 kg**

Press:
**12–16 kg**

Snatch:
**12 kg**

Load progression remains movement-specific.

---

# 20. Weight Ownership vs Weight Readiness

BellGrinda must distinguish:

**Available Weight**

from

**Approved Working Weight**

Example:

User owns:

- 12 kg.
- 16 kg.
- 20 kg.

But BellGrinda may store:

Overhead Press:
**Approved Working Load: 16 kg**

Snatch:
**Approved Working Load: 12 kg**

This prevents equipment ownership from automatically determining prescription.

---

# 21. Advanced Double Progression

Double progression becomes a standard BellGrinda mechanism.

Example:

20 kg goblet squat range:

**8–12**

Progress:

8  
→ 9  
→ 10  
→ 11  
→ 12

Then BellGrinda may progress another variable.

If no heavier kettlebell exists:

Possible next steps:

- tempo;
- pause;
- unilateral variation;
- increased density.

---

# 22. Progression Variable Hierarchy

BellGrinda should usually consider progression in this order:

1. Movement quality.
2. Repetition completion.
3. Stable RPE.
4. Load.
5. Exercise variation.
6. Density.
7. Complex difficulty.

The exact hierarchy may vary by movement.

For technical movements, variation complexity should not progress before movement quality.

---

# 23. No-Weight-Available Progression

If the user has reached the available kettlebell limit:

BellGrinda may progress through:

- unilateral variations;
- tempo;
- pauses;
- longer range of motion;
- more difficult exercise variation;
- reduced rest;
- complexes;
- density.

This allows continued progression without requiring immediate equipment purchases.

---

# 24. Plateau Detection

BellGrinda shall detect possible plateaus.

Example criteria:

- same load;
- same reps;
- similar RPE;
- no meaningful improvement;
- 3–4 relevant exposures.

A plateau does not automatically mean more intensity is needed.

Possible responses:

- change variation;
- change rep range;
- temporarily reduce workload;
- introduce a heavier load;
- increase recovery;
- alter exercise priority.

---

# 25. Plateau Classification

BellGrinda may classify plateaus as:

## Load Plateau

Cannot progress load.

## Rep Plateau

Cannot increase repetitions.

## Technique Plateau

Performance limited by movement quality.

## Recovery Plateau

Performance stagnates with elevated fatigue.

## Equipment Plateau

Current equipment limits progression.

## Motivation/Consistency Plateau

Training exposure is insufficient to judge physical progression.

AI may explain plateau classification but deterministic rules remain authoritative.

---

# 26. Deload / Recovery Week

Advanced introduces planned reduction periods.

A deload may be triggered by:

- elevated RPE trend;
- declining performance;
- repeated Yellow Days;
- high football workload;
- high soreness;
- accumulated fatigue.

Possible deload modifications:

- reduce reps by 20–30%;
- reduce rounds;
- increase rest;
- use easier variations;
- remove complexes;
- reduce ballistic work.

Deloading should not be treated as regression.

---

# 27. Readiness System

Advanced shall use the Green / Yellow / Red system introduced in Intermediate.

Inputs may include:

- sleep;
- energy;
- soreness;
- pain;
- football status;
- previous-session RPE;
- recent workload.

## Green

Normal prescription.

## Yellow

Modify one or two variables.

## Red

Recovery-focused session.

---

# 28. Readiness Trend

BellGrinda shall also track readiness trends.

Example:

Yellow

This should be treated differently from:

Green  
Green  
Green  
Yellow  
Green

Repeated Yellow/Red states may trigger a recovery-focused block adjustment.

---

# 29. Advanced Push-Up Progression

Advanced push-up work should emphasize strength and difficulty rather than high repetition counts.

Potential variations:

- Tempo decline.
- Pause decline.
- Deep-range push-up.
- Explosive push-up.
- Archer-style progression where appropriate.
- Uneven push-up.
- Other approved bodyweight pressing progressions.

Push-up selection should remain conservative around:

- shoulder discomfort;
- wrist discomfort;
- elbow discomfort.

---

# 30. Push-Up Load Equivalence

BellGrinda should not directly compare:

20 standard push-ups

with

10 difficult decline-tempo push-ups

as if the first is automatically superior.

The system should store:

- variation;
- reps;
- difficulty category;
- RPE.

This prevents misleading progression metrics.

---

# 31. Advanced Chest Development

Chest development remains a secondary goal.

Advanced may combine:

- harder push-ups;
- floor press;
- tempo work;
- paused reps;
- mechanical variation.

BellGrinda should maintain sufficient pressing volume without allowing chest work to interfere with:

- athleticism;
- shoulder health;
- football performance;
- recovery.

---

# 32. Advanced Complex System

Advanced complexes shall have predefined difficulty tiers.

## Tier 1

Three movements.

Example:

Clean  
→ Press  
→ Reverse lunge.

## Tier 2

Four movements.

Example:

Clean  
→ Press  
→ Front squat  
→ Reverse lunge.

## Tier 3

More demanding combinations or heavier loads.

Complex progression should use approved templates rather than AI-generated random movement strings.

---

# 33. Complex Selection

BellGrinda selects complex tier based on:

- Training Level.
- technique.
- equipment.
- RPE history.
- pain status.
- previous complex performance.

Complexes must remain compatible with approximately 40-minute sessions.

---

# 34. Advanced Conditioning System

Conditioning may progress through:

- skipping density;
- swing density;
- complex work;
- reduced transition time;
- circuit pace;
- football conditioning.

BellGrinda shall avoid stacking excessive conditioning on top of high-intensity football weeks.

---

# 35. Football Load Classification

Advanced introduces clearer football-load states.

## Low

Light recreational session.

## Moderate

Normal five-a-side session.

## High

Long/intense match or multiple matches.

## Competition

Tournament/competitive match.

BellGrinda may use these states to modify Thursday and Friday workloads.

---

# 36. Competition Week Adjustment

When competition load is high:

BellGrinda may:

- reduce Thursday lower-body volume;
- reduce Thursday ballistic volume;
- replace Friday workout with activation;
- reduce complex density.

The goal is performance, not maintaining artificial workout volume.

---

# 37. Exercise Fatigue Score

BellGrinda may maintain an internal fatigue cost for exercise categories.

Example:

Low:

- Dead bug.
- Glute bridge.
- Easy row.

Moderate:

- Goblet squat.
- Press.
- Lunge.

High:

- Heavy complexes.
- High-volume swings.
- Snatches.
- Hard conditioning.

The system can use this when building a week.

---

# 38. Weekly Fatigue Budget

Advanced may introduce a simple internal fatigue budget.

BellGrinda should avoid programming:

High-fatigue Monday

- high-fatigue Tuesday
- high-fatigue Wednesday
- high-fatigue Thursday
- football Friday.

The weekly structure should alternate training stress.

---

# 39. Advanced Performance Test Philosophy

Advanced tests should reflect current priorities.

There should no longer be one completely identical test for every Advanced block.

Tests shall include:

## Core Tests

Used for historical comparison.

Examples:

- Standard push-up maximum.
- Goblet squat capacity.
- Press capacity.
- Row capacity.

## Priority Test

Specific to the current block.

Examples:

Pressing block:
Overhead press test.

Conditioning block:
Work-capacity circuit.

Lower-body block:
Squat/lunge capacity.

---

# 40. Historical Benchmark Preservation

Some tests should remain standardized so BellGrinda can compare progress across levels.

Examples:

- Standard push-up max.
- Selected goblet squat benchmark.
- Selected row benchmark.
- Standardized conditioning circuit.

Advanced testing should therefore combine:

**fixed historical tests + current priority tests**

# 42. Advanced Completion

Advanced should not automatically end after one block.

The user may remain Advanced through multiple adaptive blocks.

Example:

Advanced Block 1:
Pressing emphasis.

Advanced Block 2:
Conditioning emphasis.

Advanced Block 3:
Power emphasis.

The user remains at the same Training Level while Performance Grade and category performance evolve.

---

# 43. Advancement Toward Grandmaster

Grandmaster shall not be awarded simply because a fixed number of weeks passed.

Unlike previous levels, Grandmaster should require stronger evidence of broad capability.

Potential readiness indicators:

- sustained consistency;
- strong overall Performance Grade;
- no major unresolved weak category;
- stable recovery;
- meaningful load progression;
- high-quality complex execution;
- good conditioning;
- appropriate pain management.

The exact Grandmaster requirements shall be defined in the Grandmaster Framework.

---

# 44. Advanced Repeat Logic

There is no simple “repeat Phase 3 forever” structure.

Instead:

At block completion:

BellGrinda performs:

**Assess → Identify next priority → Build next Advanced block**

Possible outcomes:

- continue same priority;
- switch primary priority;
- recovery block;
- Grandmaster readiness review.

---

# 45. AI Role in Advanced

AI becomes more useful at Advanced because more historical data exists.

AI may:

- explain why a priority was selected;
- summarize plateau patterns;
- explain load changes;
- compare current and previous blocks;
- summarize recovery patterns;
- explain performance-test results.

AI shall not independently design unrestricted training blocks.

The deterministic Training Engine chooses from approved programming rules and templates.

---

# 46. Example AI Explanation

Example:

“Your last nine weeks show strong lower-body progression and stable conditioning, but overhead pressing has remained at 16 kg × 5–6 reps with no meaningful improvement across four exposures. Your next Advanced block therefore prioritizes pressing strength while maintaining lower-body capacity.”

AI is explaining a deterministic decision.

# 49. Advanced Success Criteria

An Advanced block is successful when it produces one or more meaningful improvements without creating unacceptable fatigue or persistent pain.

Success may include:

- increased load;
- improved repetitions;
- harder movement variation;
- improved density;
- improved conditioning;
- improved category Performance Grade;
- improved recovery;
- improved consistency.

Not every metric must improve simultaneously.

---

# 50. Advanced Failure Criteria

A block should be considered poorly configured when:

- RPE remains excessively high;
- performance declines consistently;
- pain increases;
- consistency collapses due to workload;
- football performance is repeatedly compromised;
- no priority-specific improvement occurs despite adequate adherence.

BellGrinda should then modify the next block rather than blindly increasing workload.

---

# 51. Long-Term Advanced Loop

The Advanced system shall operate as:

**Review Historical Data**

↓

**Identify Priority**

↓

**Generate 6–9 Week Block**

↓

**Train**

↓

**Biweekly Assessments**

↓

**Block Performance Test**

↓

**Advanced Block Report**

↓

**Choose Next Priority**

↓

**Repeat**

This loop continues until Grandmaster readiness criteria are satisfied.

---

# 52. Advanced Design Principle

Advanced BellGrinda should feel increasingly personalized without becoming unpredictable.

The user should always be able to understand:

- why an exercise was chosen;
- why a load changed;
- why reps changed;
- why a workout was reduced;
- why a priority changed.

No major programming change should appear arbitrary.

---

# 53. Transition to Grandmaster

The next specification should define:

# BellGrinda Grandmaster Framework

Grandmaster should not simply be:

**Advanced but harder.**

It should represent demonstrated long-term capability across:

- strength;
- athleticism;
- conditioning;
- consistency;
- kettlebell skill;
- movement quality;
- recovery management;
- adaptive training discipline.

Grandmaster therefore requires its own entry criteria, maintenance logic, challenge system, and long-term progression model.
