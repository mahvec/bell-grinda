# BellGrinda Intermediate Training Specification

**Version:** 0.1  
**Training Level:** Intermediate  
**Duration:** 9 weeks  
**Phases:** 3  
**Phase Duration:** 3 weeks each  
**Training Days:** Monday–Friday  
**Session Duration:** Approximately 40 minutes  
**Default Structure:** 5 exercise stations × 5 rounds, with selected complex-based exceptions  
**Primary Equipment:** 12 kg kettlebell, recommended 16 kg kettlebell where available  
**Future Load Option:** 20 kg kettlebell where exercise-specific readiness exists  
**Secondary Equipment:** Skipping rope, mat, chair/bench

---

# 1. Intermediate Objective

Intermediate marks the point where BellGrinda stops behaving primarily like a beginner fitness plan and starts functioning as a true adaptive training system.

The level prioritizes:

- Athletic strength.
- Work capacity.
- Strength endurance.
- Power.
- Unilateral control.
- Mixed-load training.
- Exercise-specific kettlebell progression.
- Complexes.
- Training density.
- Push-up strength progression.
- Recovery management.
- Conditioning without unnecessary fatigue.
- Improved performance under accumulated workload.

Intermediate should not simply increase repetitions from Advanced Novice.

The training system shall instead increase:

- Exercise quality.
- Exercise complexity.
- Load differentiation.
- Movement combinations.
- Unilateral demands.
- Density.
- Training decision quality.

---

# 2. Intermediate Entry

Intermediate follows completion of Advanced Novice.

Advanced Novice already introduces:

- simple kettlebell complexes;
- harder push-up variations;
- unilateral work;
- density progression;
- selective 16 kg use;
- exercise-specific load management.

Intermediate expands these principles rather than replacing them.

Recommended entry characteristics include most of the following:

- Consistent training history.
- Comfortable execution of basic kettlebell movements.
- Controlled RPE during Advanced Novice workloads.
- Ability to tolerate five-round circuits.
- Improved push-up strength.
- Some exposure to tempo/pause/decline push-ups.
- Stable unilateral lunge performance.
- Confidence with clean and press.
- Ability to execute simple complexes.
- No unresolved moderate/severe exercise pain.

The user does not need to satisfy every characteristic to enter Intermediate because Training Level remains primarily time-based.

# 3. Phase Structure

## Phase 1 — Intensification

**Weeks 1–3**

Focus:

- Transition from Advanced Novice.
- Establish Intermediate loads.
- Increase unilateral strength.
- Introduce mixed-load sessions.
- Increase controlled density.
- Build familiarity with longer complexes.

Target session RPE:

**6–7**

Occasional RPE 8 is acceptable.

---

## Phase 2 — Integration

**Weeks 4–6**

Focus:

- Combine strength and conditioning more deliberately.
- Increase kettlebell complexity.
- Introduce more advanced push-up variations.
- Increase exercise-specific load.
- Improve work capacity.
- Increase unilateral demands.

Target session RPE:

**6–8**

---

## Phase 3 — Performance

**Weeks 7–9**

Focus:

- Consolidate Intermediate capacity.
- Improve sustained performance.
- Prepare for Intermediate assessment.
- Identify Advanced-level readiness.
- Increase quality under fatigue.

Target session RPE:

**7–8**

Regular RPE 9–10 remains undesirable.

---

# 4. Intermediate Warm-Up

Approximately 5–6 minutes.

Perform:

- Bodyweight squat — 10.
- Hip hinge — 10.
- Reverse lunge — 5 per leg.
- Glute bridge — 10.
- Scapular push-up — 8.
- Shoulder circles — 10 each direction.
- Kettlebell deadlift — 8.
- Kettlebell clean — 3 per arm.
- Light press — 2 per arm.

Warm-up intensity must remain low.

The warm-up prepares movement patterns rather than becoming additional conditioning.

---

# 5. Rest Structure

Intermediate shall use rest as an intentional training variable.

## Phase 1

Between exercises:

**10–20 seconds**

Between rounds:

**45–60 seconds**

---

## Phase 2

Between exercises:

**10–15 seconds**

Between rounds:

**40–50 seconds**

---

## Phase 3

Between exercises:

**Minimal transition time where appropriate**

Between rounds:

**30–45 seconds**

Rest may always increase if:

- form deteriorates;
- breathing remains uncontrolled;
- unusual fatigue occurs;
- pain appears;
- football workload requires adjustment.

BellGrinda shall not force rest reduction simply because the user has reached Intermediate.

---

# 6. Intermediate Load Philosophy

Intermediate introduces deliberate mixed-load programming.

A single workout may contain:

- 20 kg deadlift.
- 16 kg goblet squat.
- 16 kg row.
- 12 kg overhead press.

if those loads are individually appropriate.

BellGrinda must never use:

**User owns 20 kg → every exercise becomes 20 kg**

Load remains exercise-specific, consistent with the BellGrinda progression philosophy.

# 12. Intermediate Push-Up System

Intermediate should stop treating push-ups as a simple repetition target.

BellGrinda shall classify push-up difficulty by stage.

## Stage 1

Standard.

## Stage 2

Tempo.

## Stage 3

Pause.

## Stage 4

Decline.

## Stage 5

Decline + pause/tempo.

## Stage 6

Optional explosive variation.

Push-up progression shall depend on:

- successful repetitions;
- RPE;
- pressing workload;
- wrist comfort;
- shoulder comfort;
- elbow comfort;
- weekly football/training stress.

---

# 13. Chest Development Rule

Because increased chest size remains one of the user's goals, BellGrinda shall maintain meaningful horizontal pressing volume.

Intermediate chest work may include:

- Standard push-ups.
- Tempo push-ups.
- Pause push-ups.
- Decline push-ups.
- Kettlebell floor press.

However, chest work shall not dominate programming to the detriment of athleticism or recovery.

# 15. 16 kg → 20 kg Progression

The 20 kg kettlebell should first be considered for movements with high strength capacity and lower technical demand.

Likely earlier candidates:

- Deadlift.
- Goblet squat.
- Suitcase carry/march.
- Row.

Later candidates:

- Swing.
- Clean.

Very conservative candidates:

- Overhead press.
- Clean and press.
- Snatch.

BellGrinda shall not require 20 kg use for Intermediate completion.

---

# 16. Load Transition Rule

When moving to a heavier kettlebell, BellGrinda should initially reduce prescribed reps.

General guideline:

**15–35% reduction**

depending on:

- load increase;
- movement complexity;
- unilateral/bilateral pattern;
- RPE history.

Example:

16 kg goblet squat × 15

may become:

20 kg × 8–10.

---

# 17. Double Progression

Intermediate may introduce simple double progression.

Example goblet squat range:

**10–15 reps**

At 16 kg:

Session progression:

10  
→ 11  
→ 12  
→ 13  
→ 14  
→ 15

After consistent upper-range performance:

Consider 20 kg.

Then reps return to approximately:

**8–10**

This becomes easier to implement programmatically than arbitrary AI changes.

---

# 18. Density Progression

BellGrinda may improve workout density through:

- slightly reduced rest;
- smoother transitions;
- improved complex execution;
- better work completion within 40 minutes.

Density should not be increased by compromising form.

BellGrinda should calculate:

**Training Work ÷ Session Duration**

as one possible internal density metric.

The value need not always be displayed to the user.

---

# 19. Complex Progression Rules

Complexes may progress through:

- additional movement.
- additional repetition.
- heavier load.
- reduced rest.

Only one major complex variable should normally change at once.

If complex quality falls:

1. Reduce repetitions.
2. Increase rest.
3. Reduce load.
4. Simplify complex.

in that general order where appropriate.

---

# 20. RPE Rules

## RPE 1–3

Underloaded.

Progression may be considered.

## RPE 4–5

Moderate.

Usually appropriate for lighter Intermediate sessions.

## RPE 6–7

Primary productive range.

## RPE 8

Hard but acceptable.

Do not automatically progress.

## RPE 9

Near limit.

Hold or regress where repeated.

## RPE 10

Not desirable for ordinary Intermediate sessions.

Regression or recovery adjustment may be required.

---

# 21. Recovery Status

Intermediate introduces a simple readiness state.

Before training, BellGrinda may ask:

- Energy.
- Soreness.
- Sleep quality.
- Pain/discomfort.
- Football status.

The deterministic engine classifies the day.

## Green

Normal training.

## Yellow

Reduce one variable.

Examples:

- reps;
- load;
- rounds;
- density.

## Red

Recovery/substitution session.

AI may explain the classification but shall not override it.

---

# 22. Yellow-Day Adjustment

BellGrinda should avoid rebuilding the entire workout.

A Yellow Day modifies approximately one or two variables.

Example:

Normal:

16 kg goblet squat × 12.

Yellow:

16 kg × 9–10.

or

5 rounds → 4 rounds.

or

45-second rest → 60 seconds.

---

# 23. Red-Day Adjustment

Red status may result from:

- significant soreness;
- moderate pain;
- poor recovery;
- unusually high accumulated workload;
- competition demands.

Possible actions:

- mobility/recovery circuit;
- easier substitutions;
- reduced rounds;
- no ballistic work.

A Red Day completed as prescribed still counts as a successful BellGrinda training session.

---

# 24. Pain Rules

Pain logic continues to override progression.

## Mild, isolated

Record and monitor.

## Mild, repeated

Flag and modify.

## Moderate

Stop associated exercise.

## Severe

Stop movement and recommend appropriate professional evaluation.

Intermediate status never permits BellGrinda to ignore pain.

---

# 25. Swing-Specific Rule

If lower-back discomfort remains unresolved:

BellGrinda shall not:

- increase swing load;
- increase swing complexity;
- introduce high-volume swing challenges.

Possible replacements include:

- Deadlift.
- Romanian-style kettlebell hinge where approved.
- Glute bridge.
- Other approved hinge regression.

---

# 26. Snatch Introduction

Because the user already has some familiarity with kettlebell snatches, Intermediate may introduce low-volume snatch work.

Snatch should not automatically appear in every user's program.

For BellGrinda V1:

Snatches may be offered when:

- swing mechanics are stable;
- clean mechanics are stable;
- shoulder comfort is good;
- no recurring back discomfort exists;
- user opts into the movement.

Example:

Phase 1:
No programmed snatch required.

Phase 2:
**3–5 per arm**

Phase 3:
**5–6 per arm**

Snatches should replace another ballistic exercise rather than simply add more volume.

---

# 27. Intermediate Exercise Rotation

Core movements should remain recognizable.

Core pool:

- Goblet squat.
- Row.
- Push-up.
- Press.
- Hinge.
- Reverse lunge.
- Clean.
- Swing where appropriate.

Rotating movements may include:

- Floor press.
- Front-rack lunge.
- Suitcase march.
- Dead bug progression.
- Decline push-up.
- Complex variations.
- Optional snatch.

The program remains hybrid:

**stable core + controlled rotation**

rather than random daily exercise generation.

---

# 28. Missed Session Rules

## One Missed Session

Continue schedule.

No doubling.

## Two Missed Sessions

Maintain current progression variables.

## Three or More Missed Sessions

BellGrinda may reduce workload upon return.

## Break Longer Than Seven Days

Return with:

- reduced reps;
- longer rest;
- approximately one-phase temporary regression if required.

Training Level does not automatically reset.

---

# 29. Football Competition Logic

Intermediate programming must explicitly recognize football as high-intensity athletic work.

If a competition match occurs:

- Friday BellGrinda workload reduces.
- Previous Thursday workload may also be reduced if necessary.
- No attempt should be made to “make up” missed conditioning.

Football counts toward the user's athletic workload.

**20 controlled reps**

**10 controlled reps per arm**

**6 controlled reps per arm**

---

# 35. Test 4 — One-Arm Row

**12 controlled reps per side**

# 36. Test 5 — Intermediate Work-Capacity Circuit

Perform:

**4 rounds**

1. Goblet squat — 10.
2. Push-up — 10.
3. Swing — 15.
4. Row — 8 per arm.
5. Clean and press — 4 per arm.

- load used;
- technique degradation;
- completion quality.

---

# 37. Test 6 — Recovery Indicator

BellGrinda records:

- heart-rate recovery if manually available in a future version;
- otherwise perceived breathing recovery.

V1 simple method:

After conditioning circuit:

Rate recovery after approximately 2 minutes:

- Poor.
- Fair.
- Good.
- Excellent.

This is subjective but useful as historical context.

# 41. Intermediate Success Criteria

Intermediate is considered successful when the user demonstrates most of the following:

- Strong training consistency.
- Controlled five-round work capacity.
- Improved exercise-specific loading.
- Successful mixed-load programming.
- Better unilateral strength.
- Improved push-up strength/difficulty.
- Comfortable simple complexes.
- Improved conditioning.
- Ability to train effectively without chasing maximal RPE.
- Appropriate recovery management.
- Low incidence of unresolved concerning pain.

No single criterion is mandatory for time-based progression.

# 43. Transition Beyond Intermediate

BellGrinda should not immediately prescribe one rigid Advanced program.

At this point the system should have accumulated approximately:

- Novice training history.
- Advanced Novice history.
- Intermediate history.
- Multiple assessments.
- Performance tests.
- Exercise-specific load history.
- Push-up progression history.
- RPE history.
- Pain/discomfort data.
- Consistency data.
- Football workload.
- Equipment changes.

The PRD already specifies that detailed Advanced and Grandmaster programming should evolve from accumulated user data rather than being falsely predetermined months in advance.

Therefore, the next BellGrinda specification should define:

# Advanced Progression Framework

rather than a rigid Advanced workout calendar.

That framework should determine how BellGrinda uses the user's actual Novice → Intermediate history to build the next training cycle.
