# BellGrinda Novice Training Specification

**Version:** 0.1  
**Training Level:** Novice  
**Duration:** 6 weeks  
**Phases:** 3  
**Phase Duration:** 2 weeks each  
**Training Days:** Monday–Friday  
**Session Duration:** Approximately 40 minutes total  
**Default Structure:** 5 exercises × 5 rounds  
**Primary Equipment:** 12 kg kettlebell + bodyweight  
**Secondary Equipment:** Skipping rope, mat, chair/bench

---

# 1. Novice Level Objective

The Novice Level exists to build the foundation required for later BellGrinda progression.

The level prioritizes:

1. Training consistency.
2. Exercise technique.
3. General athleticism.
4. Strength endurance.
5. Cardiovascular conditioning.
6. Work capacity.
7. Push-up development.
8. Kettlebell familiarity.
9. Recovery awareness.

The Novice Level is deliberately not designed around maximal effort.

The goal is to establish a training workload that can be sustained five days per week.

---

# 2. Novice Phase Structure

## Phase 1 — Foundation

**Weeks 1–2**

Focus:

- Learn consistent movement patterns.
- Build workout habit.
- Establish realistic RPE.
- Avoid excessive soreness.
- Develop safe kettlebell technique.
- Establish baseline performance.

Target session RPE:

**4–6/10**

---

## Phase 2 — Development

**Weeks 3–4**

Focus:

- Increase training workload gradually.
- Increase conditioning.
- Improve push-up endurance.
- Introduce more challenging kettlebell movements.
- Reduce unnecessary rest.
- Improve exercise quality under fatigue.

Target session RPE:

**5–7/10**

---

## Phase 3 — Consolidation

**Weeks 5–6**

Focus:

- Stabilize workload.
- Improve work capacity.
- Prepare for Novice Performance Test.
- Identify strengths and weaknesses.
- Prepare for Advanced Novice.

Target session RPE:

**6–7/10**

Occasional RPE 8 sessions are acceptable.

Consistent RPE 8–10 is not the objective.

---

# 3. Standard Session Structure

Every normal session should target approximately 40 minutes.

## Warm-Up

Approximately 5–6 minutes.

## Main Circuit

Approximately 28–30 minutes.

## Cooldown

Approximately 4–5 minutes.

The system should prioritize session completion over exact timing.

---

# 4. Novice Warm-Up

BellGrinda shall use a simple repeatable warm-up.

Perform:

- Bodyweight squat — 10 reps.
- Hip hinge drill — 10 reps.
- Alternating reverse lunge — 5 reps per leg.
- Arm circles — 10 forward + 10 backward.
- Glute bridge — 10 reps.
- Incline or easy push-ups — 5 reps.
- Light kettlebell deadlift — 10 reps.

The goal is preparation rather than fatigue.

---

# 5. Rest Rules

## Between Exercises

Phase 1:

**20–30 seconds when needed.**

Phase 2:

**15–25 seconds.**

Phase 3:

**10–20 seconds where appropriate.**

## Between Rounds

Phase 1:

**60–90 seconds.**

Phase 2:

**60 seconds.**

Phase 3:

**45–60 seconds.**

Rest may be extended when:

- Technique deteriorates.
- RPE rises excessively.
- Breathing remains uncontrolled.
- Discomfort appears.

BellGrinda shall not reward unsafe rest reduction.

# 13. Push-Up Progression Rules

Push-up progression shall consider:

- Completion.
- Form.
- RPE.
- Total weekly pressing volume.
- Shoulder/wrist/elbow discomfort.

## Stage A

Standard push-ups.

Target:

**20 reps**

## Stage B

Controlled tempo.

Example:

**3 seconds down + normal press**

Target may drop to:

**10–15 reps**

## Stage C

Pause push-up.

Short pause near bottom.

## Stage D

Decline push-up.

Feet elevated.

## Stage E

More advanced approved variations.

BellGrinda shall not automatically increase:

20 → 25 → 30 → 40

simply because the user becomes stronger.

---

# 14. Push-Up Failure Handling

If the prescribed target is 20 but completed repetitions across rounds are:

20  
20  
16  
13  
10

BellGrinda records the actual values.

This is not automatically considered failure.

The progression engine should analyze:

- total volume;
- form;
- RPE;
- fatigue curve.

The following workout may maintain rather than increase difficulty.

---

# 15. Exercise Progression Principle

BellGrinda shall generally change only **one major progression variable at a time**.

Examples:

Increase reps.

OR

Increase load.

OR

Introduce harder variation.

OR

Reduce rest.

Avoid:

Higher weight + more reps + harder variation + shorter rest simultaneously.

---

# 16. Rep Progression Rule

For an exercise:

If the user completes all prescribed work with:

- good form;
- no relevant pain;
- RPE approximately ≤6;
- similar performance across multiple exposures;

BellGrinda may increase repetitions within the exercise's approved range.

Example:

Goblet squat:

12 → 14 → 15.

Once the upper range becomes comfortable, load progression may be considered.

---

# 17. 12 kg → 16 kg Progression

When a 16 kg kettlebell becomes available, each exercise shall be evaluated separately.

A global equipment upgrade is prohibited.

---

## Lower-Body Exercises

Example:

12 kg goblet squat × 15

may transition to:

16 kg × 10–12.

---

## Deadlift

Because deadlift is generally a strong movement:

12 kg × 18

may progress relatively early to:

16 kg × 12–15.

---

## Row

12 kg × 12 per arm

may transition to:

16 kg × approximately 8–10 per arm.

---

## Overhead Press

12 kg pressing should remain until:

- all sets are completed consistently;
- technique remains stable;
- RPE is controlled;
- no shoulder discomfort exists.

The jump from 12 to 16 kg represents approximately a 33% load increase.

Therefore, press progression should be conservative.

---

## Swing

Swing load shall not increase while recurring lower-back discomfort is present.

---

## Clean / Snatch

Technical kettlebell movements should progress more slowly than simpler strength movements.

Load increases require:

- stable technique;
- controlled RPE;
- confidence;
- no relevant discomfort.

---

# 18. Future 20 kg Progression

The same exercise-specific principle shall apply to 20 kg.

Owning a 20 kg kettlebell does not mean every movement should use it.

BellGrinda may simultaneously prescribe:

20 kg goblet squat  
16 kg row  
12 kg press

depending on performance.

---

# 19. RPE Progression Logic

## RPE 1–3

Workout/exercise may be too easy.

BellGrinda may consider progression.

---

## RPE 4–6

Target Novice range.

Maintain or gradually progress.

---

## RPE 7

Challenging but acceptable.

Usually maintain.

---

## RPE 8

Do not automatically increase difficulty.

Review performance.

---

## RPE 9

Hold progression or reduce workload.

---

## RPE 10

Treat as excessive for normal Novice training.

Next exposure should generally regress.

---

# 20. RPE Trend Rule

BellGrinda should not modify programming based solely on one unusual session.

Example:

One RPE 9 session after poor sleep should not automatically cause major regression.

Trend-based decisions should consider approximately:

**2–3 relevant workout exposures.**

---

# 21. Pain/Discomfort Rules

Pain rules override progression rules.

## No Discomfort

Normal programming.

## Mild Discomfort — One Occurrence

Record and monitor.

Exercise may continue only if movement remains comfortable and technically controlled.

## Repeated Mild Discomfort

Flag the exercise.

Consider:

- regression;
- load reduction;
- substitute exercise;
- technique review.

## Moderate Discomfort

Stop the associated exercise for the session.

Use an approved substitute when appropriate.

## Severe Discomfort

Stop the movement.

BellGrinda should recommend appropriate medical/professional evaluation rather than attempting diagnosis.

---

# 22. Lower-Back Swing Rule

Because lower-back discomfort already exists during swings:

BellGrinda shall treat swing progression conservatively.

During Novice:

- 12 kg maximum initially.
- Low/moderate reps.
- Technique prioritized.
- No load increase while discomfort continues.

If discomfort appears repeatedly:

Swing may temporarily be replaced by:

- Kettlebell deadlift.
- Glute bridge.
- Other approved hip-hinge regressions.

---

# 23. Missed Workout Rules

BellGrinda shall not punish missed workouts.

## One Missed Session

Continue normal schedule.

Do not combine two full workouts into one day.

## Two or More Missed Sessions in One Week

BellGrinda may recommend maintaining rather than progressing the following week.

## Extended Break

After approximately 7+ days away:

BellGrinda may temporarily reduce workload.

---

# 24. Training Consistency Metric

BellGrinda shall track:

**Completed planned sessions ÷ available planned sessions**

Example:

9 completed / 10 scheduled

=

**90% consistency**

Football-adjusted sessions and approved recovery sessions still count when completed as prescribed.

# 26. Novice Performance Test

At the end of Week 6, BellGrinda offers an optional Novice Performance Test.

The test shall not be performed immediately after a hard workout.

---

## Test 1 — Maximum Clean Push-Ups

Perform one continuous set.

Stop when clean technique can no longer be maintained.

Record:

- repetitions;
- RPE;
- comparison with baseline.

---

## Test 2 — Goblet Squat Capacity

Using:

**12 kg**

Perform:

**20 controlled repetitions**

BellGrinda records:

- completion;
- RPE;
- movement confidence.

This is not an AMRAP test.

---

## Test 3 — One-Arm Row

Using:

**12 kg**

Target:

**12 clean reps per arm**

Record:

- completion;
- control;
- RPE.

---

## Test 4 — Conditioning Circuit

Perform three rounds:

1. Goblet squat — 10.
2. Push-up — 10.
3. Kettlebell deadlift — 10.
4. One-arm row — 8 per arm.
5. Skipping — 50.

Record:

- completion time;
- RPE;
- pauses;
- completion quality.

# 27. Novice Performance Grade

BellGrinda V1 shall initially use four grades:

## Developing

Significant difficulty completing Novice workload consistently.

## Competent

Can complete most prescribed work with acceptable control.

## Strong

Shows meaningful improvement in strength, endurance, and consistency.

## Excellent

Consistently exceeds Novice expectations while maintaining controlled RPE and technique.

The exact scoring algorithm should be defined separately before implementation.

---

# 28. Level Completion Decision

At the end of Novice:

BellGrinda displays:

- Training consistency.
- Push-up change.
- Kettlebell performance.
- Conditioning change.
- RPE trend.
- Pain history.
- Performance Grade.
- AI analysis.

The user may choose:

**Advance to Advanced Novice**

or

**Repeat Phase 3**

---

# 29. Repeat Phase 3 Logic

If the user repeats Phase 3:

BellGrinda identifies one or two weak areas.

Examples:

- Push strength.
- Conditioning.
- Lower-body endurance.
- Technique.
- Consistency.

The reinforcement block should emphasize those areas without unnecessarily increasing total workload.

After two weeks:

Performance Test becomes available again.

The user may:

- Advance.
- Repeat again.

---

# 30. Novice Success Criteria

The Novice Level should be considered successful if, by completion, the user demonstrates most of the following:

- Regular five-day training behavior.
- Improved exercise confidence.
- Improved push-up capacity or quality.
- Improved circuit tolerance.
- Stable/moderate average RPE.
- Better kettlebell control.
- Increased work capacity.
- Low frequency of concerning discomfort.
- Ability to complete most scheduled sessions.
- Readiness for more complex Advanced Novice programming.

The user does not need to achieve every metric to progress.
