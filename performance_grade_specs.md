# BellGrinda Performance Grade & Scoring Specification

**Version:** 0.1  
**Purpose:** Define how BellGrinda calculates Performance Grades, category scores, progression quality, and Grandmaster readiness.  
**Applies To:** Novice, Advanced Novice, Intermediate, Advanced, Grandmaster  
**Scoring Type:** Deterministic, normalized, historical, category-based  
**Score Range:** 0–100

---

# 1. Purpose

The BellGrinda Performance Grade system exists to measure the user's current demonstrated capability independently from Training Level.

Training Level represents:

- curriculum progression;
- experience;
- time spent training;
- completed training phases.

Performance Grade represents:

- current physical capability;
- training consistency;
- work capacity;
- strength;
- conditioning;
- recovery;
- movement quality;
- progression quality.

A user may therefore be:

**Training Level: Advanced**

while having:

**Performance Grade: Competent**

or:

**Training Level: Intermediate**

with:

**Performance Grade: Strong**

This distinction prevents BellGrinda from treating time spent training as automatic proof of physical mastery.

---

# 2. Overall Performance Score

BellGrinda shall maintain an Overall Performance Score from:

**0–100**

The score shall be derived from category scores rather than one isolated test.

Initial recommended categories:

1. Consistency.
2. Strength.
3. Push Strength.
4. Pull Strength.
5. Lower-Body Strength.
6. Conditioning.
7. Work Capacity.
8. Recovery.
9. Movement Quality.
10. Progression Quality.

Advanced and Grandmaster may additionally track:

11. Power.
12. Kettlebell Skill.
13. Complex Performance.
14. Training Decision Quality.

Not every category needs to contribute equally at every Training Level.

---

# 3. Performance Grade Bands

BellGrinda shall map the Overall Performance Score to four primary grades.

## Developing

**0–49**

Meaning:

The user is still building reliable capability in several important categories.

---

## Competent

**50–69**

Meaning:

The user can perform most expected work with reasonable consistency and control.

---

## Strong

**70–84**

Meaning:

The user demonstrates meaningful capability across most important categories and is progressing reliably.

---

## Excellent

**85–100**

Meaning:

The user demonstrates high capability, strong consistency, controlled effort, and broad performance quality.

Performance Grade should not imply elite athletic status outside BellGrinda.

It is an internal BellGrinda training classification.

---

# 4. Grade Interpretation Principle

BellGrinda shall avoid simplistic interpretations such as:

**80 = twice as good as 40**

The score is an internal normalized indicator.

Its purpose is to:

- track improvement;
- compare categories;
- support training decisions;
- support readiness decisions;
- identify weak areas;
- provide historical context.

---

# 5. Category Score Structure

Each category shall produce a score from:

**0–100**

Example:

Consistency: 92  
Strength: 76  
Push Strength: 68  
Pull Strength: 84  
Lower Body: 81  
Conditioning: 73  
Work Capacity: 79  
Recovery: 65  
Movement Quality: 82  
Progression Quality: 77

BellGrinda then combines these using level-specific weights.

# 11. Consistency Score

Consistency shall be calculated primarily from:

**Completed prescribed sessions ÷ available prescribed sessions**

Example:

18 completed / 20 scheduled

=

**90%**

Base consistency score:

**90**

Adjustments may apply.

---

# 12. Consistency Adjustments

Approved modified sessions count as completed.

Examples:

- football-adjusted Friday;
- recovery session;
- Yellow Day modification;
- Red Day recovery prescription.

These should not reduce consistency simply because they were easier.

Abandoned workouts may count partially.

Recommended rule:

- Full completed session: 100%.
- Approved reduced/recovery session: 100%.
- Partially completed session: proportional.
- Abandoned without meaningful completion: 0%.
- Missed session: 0%.

---

# 13. Consistency Window

BellGrinda should calculate:

- current 14-day consistency;
- current block consistency;
- lifetime consistency.

Performance Grade should primarily use:

**current block / recent period**

rather than lifetime data.

Historical consistency remains visible separately.

---

# 14. Strength Score

Strength Score should not be based on one absolute kettlebell weight.

It should combine:

- exercise-specific load;
- repetitions;
- movement difficulty;
- historical improvement;
- controlled RPE.

Relevant exercises may include:

- goblet squat;
- deadlift;
- overhead press;
- row;
- floor press;
- loaded lunge.

---

# 15. Strength Normalization

BellGrinda should use exercise-specific reference bands.

Example concept:

For Goblet Squat:

12 kg × 10 may score lower than:

16 kg × 10

which may score lower than:

20 kg × 10.

However:

20 kg × 3 at RPE 10 should not automatically score better than:

16 kg × 12 at RPE 7.

Therefore Strength Score should consider:

**Load × Reps × Quality Factor**

rather than load alone.

---

# 16. Exercise Strength Index

BellGrinda may calculate an internal Exercise Strength Index:

**Load Factor × Rep Factor × Quality Factor**

Conceptually:

Exercise Strength Index = normalized load × normalized reps × quality multiplier.

Quality multiplier may consider:

- RPE;
- form confidence;
- pain status;
- completion consistency.

Exact coefficients should be calibrated later.

---

# 17. Push Strength Score

Push Strength should consider:

- max standard push-ups;
- current push-up variation;
- reps performed;
- RPE;
- floor press;
- overhead press.

Standard push-ups remain useful because they provide historical continuity from the original baseline.

Harder variations provide additional difficulty data.

---

# 18. Push-Up Difficulty Multipliers

BellGrinda may assign internal difficulty multipliers.

Example initial model:

- Standard push-up: 1.00
- Tempo push-up: 1.10
- Pause push-up: 1.10
- Close-grip push-up: 1.10
- Decline push-up: 1.20
- Decline tempo/pause: 1.30
- Explosive push-up: 1.25
- Archer-style progression: 1.40

These are internal BellGrinda comparison values, not scientific claims.

They should be calibrated through usage.

---

# 19. Push Performance Index

Possible internal formula:

**Completed Reps × Difficulty Multiplier × Quality Modifier**

Example:

20 standard push-ups:

20 × 1.00

=

20 points raw.

10 decline-tempo push-ups:

10 × 1.30

=

13 raw points.

This raw index should then be normalized against the user's historical performance and BellGrinda level expectations.

---

# 20. Pull Strength Score

Pull Strength shall primarily use:

- one-arm row;
- load;
- reps;
- control;
- RPE;
- symmetry between arms.

BellGrinda should detect substantial left/right asymmetry where data exists.

Example:

Right arm:
16 kg × 12.

Left arm:
16 kg × 7.

This should reduce the Pull Strength category score relative to balanced performance.

---

# 21. Lower-Body Strength Score

Lower-body strength should combine:

- goblet squat;
- reverse lunge;
- front-rack lunge;
- deadlift;
- unilateral squat/lunge performance;
- available load.

The score should reflect both:

- bilateral strength;
- unilateral control.

---

# 22. Conditioning Score

Conditioning should use:

- circuit completion;
- skipping performance;
- football workload;
- circuit time;
- rest taken;
- perceived recovery;
- RPE.

BellGrinda should avoid equating:

**harder suffering**

with

**better conditioning**

Improved conditioning is more appropriately reflected by:

- equal work at lower RPE;
- more work at equal RPE;
- faster completion with controlled technique;
- faster recovery.

---

# 23. Conditioning Improvement Signals

Examples:

Same circuit:

Week 1:
24 minutes, RPE 8.

Week 6:
20 minutes, RPE 7.

This represents strong improvement.

Another example:

Week 1:
3 rounds.

Week 6:
5 rounds at similar RPE.

Also represents improvement.

---

# 24. Work Capacity Score

Work Capacity measures how much useful work the user can sustain.

Inputs:

- rounds completed;
- reps completed;
- total load;
- workout duration;
- rest;
- RPE.

Conceptual metric:

**Useful Work / Time**

BellGrinda may calculate an internal training-density score.

---

# 25. Work Capacity vs Conditioning

These categories overlap but are not identical.

Conditioning:

“How well can the cardiovascular system support training?”

Work Capacity:

“How much total productive work can be performed and recovered from?”

A user may have:

Strong conditioning

but

Competent work capacity

if muscular fatigue limits performance.

---

# 26. Recovery Score

Recovery Score should use trends rather than a single day's feeling.

Inputs may include:

- readiness status;
- soreness;
- sleep quality;
- repeated Yellow Days;
- repeated Red Days;
- elevated RPE;
- performance decline;
- workout completion;
- football load.

---

# 27. Recovery Score Signals

Positive:

- Mostly Green Days.
- Stable performance.
- Low unexplained RPE spikes.
- Appropriate deload response.
- Stable consistency.

Negative:

- Frequent Yellow/Red Days.
- Performance decline.
- High RPE across multiple sessions.
- Persistent soreness.
- Repeated missed sessions linked to fatigue.

---

# 28. Movement Quality Score

Movement Quality should consider:

- exercise completion with stable form;
- reported technique confidence;
- absence of repeated form-related substitutions;
- pain/discomfort;
- later video-form analysis when available.

For V1, Movement Quality will rely mainly on:

- user self-report;
- pain flags;
- exercise completion;
- exercise regression history.

---

# 29. Progression Quality Score

Progression Quality measures whether BellGrinda progression is productive rather than reckless.

Positive signals:

- gradual load increases;
- controlled RPE;
- stable technique;
- successful rep increases;
- successful variation increases;
- low pain frequency.

Negative signals:

- repeated failed progressions;
- frequent regressions caused by overloading;
- persistent RPE 9–10;
- pain following load increases.

---

# 30. Power Score

Power becomes more relevant from Intermediate onward.

Possible inputs:

- swing performance;
- clean performance;
- snatch performance;
- explosive push-up progression;
- ballistic exercise quality.

BellGrinda should avoid pretending to measure true mechanical power without sensors.

This category represents **BellGrinda power capability**, not laboratory-measured watts.

---

# 31. Kettlebell Skill Score

Kettlebell Skill becomes increasingly important in Grandmaster.

Possible inputs:

- clean;
- swing;
- press;
- clean and press;
- snatch;
- complexes.

Consider:

- movement confidence;
- load;
- repetitions;
- technical history;
- pain flags;
- successful progressions.

---

# 32. Complex Performance Score

Complex Performance may consider:

- complex tier;
- repetitions;
- load;
- smooth transitions;
- round completion;
- RPE;
- historical improvement.

Advanced and Grandmaster may use this category more heavily.

---

# 33. Training Decision Quality

Grandmaster may track whether the user responds appropriately to training signals.

Positive examples:

- accepting recovery sessions when needed;
- not forcing heavier loads after pain;
- completing deloads;
- using approved substitutions;
- not repeatedly overriding Red readiness states.

This score should remain low-weighted.

It exists to support long-term training behavior, not punish the user.

---

# 34. Pain Handling

Pain/discomfort shall affect category scoring carefully.

Pain should not directly reduce the Overall Performance Score simply because discomfort occurred.

Instead, BellGrinda should distinguish:

**Pain occurrence**

from

**Pain management**

Example:

A user reports knee discomfort, stops lunges, uses an approved substitute, and completes a modified workout.

BellGrinda should not heavily penalize this.

Poor pain management would be:

- repeated continued loading;
- ignoring severe pain;
- repeated unsuccessful progression.

---

# 35. Safety Modifier

BellGrinda may apply a Safety Modifier where necessary.

Example:

Performance Score before modifier:

82.

Persistent moderate pain ignored across multiple sessions:

Safety Modifier:

−5.

Adjusted Score:

77.

This modifier should only apply when meaningful unsafe training behavior occurs.

It must not punish users simply for reporting discomfort honestly.

---

# 36. Pain Transparency Rule

BellGrinda should never encourage users to hide pain to protect their score.

Therefore:

Reporting discomfort:

**No direct negative score penalty.**

Ignoring safety recommendations:

Potential negative impact.

This is critical.

---

# 37. RPE Modifier

RPE should influence quality rather than dominate scores.

Example:

Same performance:

16 kg × 10 at RPE 6

should score slightly better than:

16 kg × 10 at RPE 9.

BellGrinda should interpret:

lower RPE at equal performance

as increased capacity.

---

# 38. RPE Quality Bands

Possible internal multiplier:

RPE 4–6:
**1.00**

RPE 7:
**0.98**

RPE 8:
**0.95**

RPE 9:
**0.90**

RPE 10:
**0.85**

RPE 1–3 should not automatically increase score because the exercise may simply be underloaded.

Exact values should be calibrated later.

---

# 39. Historical Improvement Score

BellGrinda should reward meaningful improvement from baseline.

Example:

Push-ups:

20 → 28.

Improvement:

40%.

Historical Improvement should influence Progression Quality but should not permanently inflate Overall Performance.

A user eventually needs to demonstrate current capability, not rely forever on early beginner gains.

---

# 40. Recency Weighting

Recent data should matter more than old data.

Recommended initial weighting:

- Last 14 days: highest importance.
- Previous 15–42 days: moderate importance.
- Older data: historical reference.

This allows current Performance Grade to reflect current capability.

---

# 41. Score Smoothing

BellGrinda should not allow one bad workout to drastically change Performance Grade.

Use rolling averages.

Example:

Category Score:

60% recent block average  
30% previous block average  
10% most recent assessment

or another calibrated model.

The exact implementation may evolve.

---

# 42. Minimum Data Requirement

BellGrinda should not calculate a confident grade from insufficient data.

Possible statuses:

- Insufficient Data.
- Preliminary Grade.
- Established Grade.

Example:

After two workouts:

**Preliminary: Competent**

After four weeks:

**Established: Competent**

---

# 43. Preliminary Grade

Recommended rule:

Less than:

**6 completed sessions**

=

Preliminary.

No Grandmaster readiness decision should use Preliminary Grades.

---

# 44. Category Confidence

Each category may include a confidence indicator.

Example:

Strength:
82 — High confidence.

Power:
71 — Low confidence.

because only one ballistic session has been logged recently.

AI may explain low confidence.

---

# 45. Assessment Weighting

Performance tests should influence scores more than ordinary single sessions but should not completely override training history.

Recommended model:

- Training history: 60–70%.
- Performance test: 20–30%.
- Assessment data: 10–20%.

Exact weighting may vary by category.

---

# 46. Standardized Test Importance

Standardized tests provide historical comparability.

Examples:

- standard push-up max;
- goblet squat benchmark;
- row benchmark;
- conditioning circuit.

BellGrinda should preserve these tests across levels where possible.

---

# 47. Score Normalization Across Kettlebell Weights

BellGrinda should normalize performance when load changes.

Example:

12 kg × 15

versus

16 kg × 10

should not be compared on reps alone.

BellGrinda should maintain an internal load-performance index.

Conceptually:

**Load × Reps**

provides a simple volume proxy.

Example:

12 × 15 = 180

16 × 10 = 160

But this does not mean 12 kg performance is automatically superior.

Therefore additional factors must include:

- load difficulty;
- unilateral/bilateral status;
- RPE;
- exercise type;
- historical progression.

BellGrinda must avoid naive volume-only scoring.

---

# 48. Exercise-Specific Expectations

Each exercise should have its own scoring configuration.

Example:

Pressing:

Weight progression is slower.

Deadlift:

Weight progression may be faster.

Snatch:

Technique matters more.

Goblet squat:

Load and reps both matter.

One global scoring formula would be poor design.

---

# 49. Category Grade Bands

Each category shall use the same four labels initially:

0–49:
**Developing**

50–69:
**Competent**

70–84:
**Strong**

85–100:
**Excellent**

This creates a consistent language across the application.

---

# 50. Overall Grade Calculation

Conceptual formula:

**Overall Score = Σ(Category Score × Level Weight)**

Example Intermediate user:

Consistency:
90 × 0.12 = 10.8

Strength:
75 × 0.12 = 9.0

Push:
70 × 0.10 = 7.0

Pull:
82 × 0.10 = 8.2

Lower Body:
80 × 0.10 = 8.0

Conditioning:
72 × 0.10 = 7.2

Work Capacity:
78 × 0.12 = 9.36

Recovery:
68 × 0.10 = 6.8

Movement:
80 × 0.07 = 5.6

Progression:
75 × 0.07 = 5.25

Overall:

approximately **77**

Performance Grade:

**Strong**

---

# 51. Grade Change Rules

BellGrinda should avoid rapid grade oscillation.

Recommended:

A grade only changes after:

- enough recent data exists;
- score crosses threshold;
- threshold remains crossed for multiple relevant exposures or assessment event.

Example:

69 → 71 after one workout

should not instantly trigger:

Competent → Strong.

---

# 52. Grade Hysteresis

Use a small buffer.

Example:

To enter Strong:

Score must reach:

**72**

To remain Strong:

Score must stay above:

**68**

This reduces constant grade flipping.

Exact thresholds can be tuned later.

---

# 53. Performance Trend

BellGrinda shall show:

- improving;
- stable;
- declining.

Example:

Performance Grade:
Strong.

Trend:
↑ Improving.

This provides more information than grade alone.

---

# 54. Trend Calculation

Trend should compare:

current rolling score

vs

previous equivalent period.

Possible thresholds:

+5 or more:
Improving.

−4 to +4:
Stable.

−5 or worse:
Declining.

These should remain configurable.

---

# 55. Category Trends

Each category should have its own trend.

Example:

Strength:
Strong ↑

Conditioning:
Competent ↑

Recovery:
Competent ↓

Push:
Strong →

This makes training priorities easier to select.

---

# 56. Priority Selection Integration

Advanced programming shall use category scores and trends.

Possible priority rule:

Select the lowest meaningful category that is:

- trainable;
- not restricted by pain;
- relevant to the user's goals.

Example:

Conditioning: 82.

Push Strength: 61.

Recovery: 72.

Push Strength becomes likely priority.

---

# 57. Priority Override Rules

The lowest score should not always become the priority.

Example:

Power:
55

but user currently has recurring swing-related pain.

BellGrinda should not choose Power as primary priority if training it would require contraindicated ballistic work.

Safety overrides score optimization.

---

# 58. Grandmaster Readiness Score

Grandmaster readiness shall use more than Overall Performance Score.

Recommended requirements:

1. Advanced Training Level.
2. At least 2 completed Advanced blocks.
3. Overall Performance Grade Strong or Excellent.
4. No major category below Competent.
5. Multiple key categories Strong or Excellent.
6. Stable Recovery Grade.
7. Strong Consistency.
8. No unresolved moderate/severe pain preventing normal training.

The Advanced framework already identifies broad capability and sustained consistency as essential Grandmaster readiness signals.

---

# 59. Grandmaster Readiness Category Minimums

Recommended initial minimums:

Consistency:
**75+**

Strength:
**70+**

Conditioning:
**70+**

Work Capacity:
**70+**

Recovery:
**65+**

Movement Quality:
**70+**

No category:

below **50**

except where temporarily affected by a documented reconditioning situation.

---

# 60. Grandmaster Readiness Classification

BellGrinda shall classify readiness as:

## Ready

All primary readiness conditions satisfied.

## Almost Ready

Overall conditions strong but one or two categories fall slightly below target.

## Not Yet Ready

Multiple significant weaknesses remain.

---

# 61. Almost Ready Threshold

Example:

Overall: 78.

Consistency: 88.

Strength: 76.

Conditioning: 74.

Recovery: 62.

Everything else strong.

BellGrinda may classify:

**Almost Ready**

and generate a Recovery-focused Grandmaster Preparation Block.

---

# 62. Grandmaster Preparation Block

Preparation blocks should target one or two readiness gaps.

Duration:

approximately **3–6 weeks**

Examples:

- recovery;
- conditioning;
- pressing strength;
- movement quality;
- consistency.

Preparation does not reset Training Level.

---

# 63. Grandmaster Reconditioning

Once Grandmaster is achieved, current Performance Grade may decline without removing Training Level.

Example:

Training Level:
Grandmaster.

Current Overall Score: 63.

Performance Grade:
Competent.

Status:
Reconditioning.

This preserves long-term achievement while accurately reflecting current capacity.

---

# 64. Biweekly Assessment Score Update

Every biweekly assessment should trigger recalculation of:

- category scores;
- Overall Score;
- Performance Grade;
- trends;
- category confidence;
- Grandmaster readiness where applicable.

The AI may explain changes afterward.

---

# 65. Workout-Level Micro Scores

BellGrinda may calculate internal workout-level quality scores.

Possible inputs:

- completion;
- RPE;
- pain;
- prescribed vs actual reps;
- load;
- workout duration.

These micro scores should feed larger category trends.

They do not need to be prominently displayed.

---

# 66. Avoid Gamification Abuse

BellGrinda must not encourage:

- exercising through pain;
- skipping rest;
- maximizing RPE;
- overtraining;
- falsifying workout logs;
- avoiding recovery sessions

simply to protect a score.

The scoring system must reward sustainable training behavior.

---

# 67. Score Explanation

Every score change should be explainable.

Example:

Push Strength:

74 → 78

because:

- decline push-up improved from 8 to 10 reps;
- overhead press remained stable;
- RPE dropped from 8 to 7.

BellGrinda AI may provide this explanation, but the score itself is deterministic.

---

# 68. AI Responsibility

AI may:

- explain score changes;
- summarize category weaknesses;
- explain Grandmaster readiness;
- summarize trends;
- generate readable reports.

AI shall not:

- arbitrarily change scores;
- override scoring rules;
- promote Training Level;
- ignore safety modifiers.

---

# 69. Score Auditability

BellGrinda should retain enough underlying data to explain any current category score.

For each score, the system should be able to retrieve:

- relevant workouts;
- tests;
- assessment values;
- progression history;
- modifiers.

This is important for debugging and future commercial trust.

---

# 70. Score Versioning

Scoring rules may evolve.

BellGrinda should store:

**Scoring Model Version**

Example:

v1.0

Future recalibration should not silently rewrite historical scores without traceability.

Historical records should retain the scoring model used.

---

# 71. Initial Calibration Strategy

Because BellGrinda V1 begins with one user, scoring thresholds should initially be considered:

**provisional**

The system should record raw underlying metrics.

After sufficient usage, thresholds can be recalibrated.

This is preferable to pretending the first scoring model is scientifically perfect.

---

# 81. Success Criteria

The Performance Grade system is successful if:

- scores respond meaningfully to real progress;
- one bad workout does not destroy the score;
- pain reporting is not punished;
- harder exercise variations are recognized;
- heavier loads are normalized sensibly;
- consistency matters;
- recovery matters;
- scores remain explainable;
- Training Level remains distinct from capability;
- Grandmaster readiness cannot be earned through time alone.

---

# 82. Known Limitations

BellGrinda V1 does not possess:

- laboratory strength testing;
- force plates;
- VO2 max testing;
- verified body-composition analysis;
- professional movement assessment;
- wearable-derived recovery metrics.

Therefore BellGrinda scores must be presented as:

**training-system indicators**

not clinical or scientific measurements.
